Palindromic cell closures

Three-row Springer fibers. A cell's closure is palindromic when its Poincaré polynomial reads the same forwards and backwards — the closure is Poincaré-dual. Closures are the transitive closure of the recorded degenerations, so each contains the closed point.

How this is computed

For each cell the closure is the transitive closure of the recorded degenerations, so it is genuinely closed and always contains the unique 0-dimensional cell. Grading its members by dimension gives the Poincaré polynomial; the cell is listed here when that polynomial is palindromic. Because the bottom coefficient is pinned at 1 by the unique closed point, symmetry forces the top coefficient to be 1 as well — so a cell dominating any other cell of its own dimension can never appear.
